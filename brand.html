leaf by leaf

The identity of The Times of Palestine, grown from the three leaves of its logo. Take the logo away and the page is still theirs.

01 · THE MARK

one leaf, three times

The supplied mark is a single shape, a pair of leaves on one stem, stacked three times. The stack is exactly as tall as three leaves: the tip of each one sits in the notch of the next. Nothing was redrawn. The site takes the shape and the order as they are.

  • Red is today. Anything reported in the last 24 hours wears the top leaf.
  • Green is this week. Stories still growing: follow-ups, context, explainers.
  • Black is on the record. Older than a week, and still true.
  • 1 : 2.31Depth to width of one leaf. Every leaf on the site keeps it.
02 · THE SHAPES

everything is a leaf

One blade of the mark, squared up, is a rectangle with two opposite corners rounded. That is the only frame on the site: photographs, buttons, chips, boxes. Neighbours lean opposite ways, as the two blades of a leaf do. Point at one and it turns over.

The leaf

Marks every tag, subhead and section word. Its colour is the story's age. It dips when you point at the story.

The blade

Every photograph and box. Two soft corners, two firm ones. Hover turns it over.

The vine

Leaves tip to tip in a row: the border between today, this week and the record.

The stem

The latest stories hang off one line that runs red to green to black, newest at the top.

03 · COLOUR

three colours, one meaning each

The colours are the logo's own and each does one job. Colour is never decoration here: it tells the reader when.

Green · this week#0066337.1:1 on white. The logo's green. Context, links, explainers.
Ink · on record#19070719.5:1 on white. The wordmark's ink. Text, and everything older than a week.

04 · TYPE

a word and a voice

The wordmark is a fat-face Didone with ball terminals. Of ten display serifs set beside it, Abril Fatface was the closest in weight, contrast and terminals, so it carries the big lower-case words. Instrument Sans does everything else: its width axis sets headlines at 80 to 88 percent, which keeps a long headline to two lines, and at full width it reads cleanly at 19 pixels.

this week

Who was Yasser Arafat, and how did Palestinians mark the 22nd anniversary of his death this week?

Reading · Instrument Sans · width 100 · weight 400

Palestinian Vice-President Hussein al-Sheikh laid a wreath at the tomb of Yasser Arafat in the presidential compound in Ramallah on Wednesday 11 November, on behalf of President Mahmoud Abbas, WAFA reported. It was the 22nd anniversary of Arafat's death. Al-Sheikh recited verses from the Quran at the graveside. The ceremony is held every year at the mausoleum inside the compound known as the Muqata'a.

05 · MOTION

how it grows

The opening draws a stem, then the three leaves unfurl from it in order: red, green, black. Between pages a three-colour line crosses the top. In discover, a story is told in three leaves: now, why, on record. Reduced-motion settings switch all of it off.
