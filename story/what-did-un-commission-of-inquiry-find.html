What did the UN Commission of Inquiry conclude in its October report on the West Bank and Gaza?

The short answer

The commission found war crimes and crimes against humanity in the West Bank and said genocidal acts continue in Gaza; Israel rejects this.

On record26 October 2026, 18:20 JerusalemNew York2 min read

The Independent International Commission of Inquiry on the Occupied Palestinian Territory, including East Jerusalem, and Israel presented its latest report to the UN General Assembly on Monday 26 October. Its headline finding is that Israel has committed war crimes and the crimes against humanity of extermination, persecution, torture, rape and other forms of sexual violence against Palestinians, and that it continues to commit genocide in Gaza after the ceasefire of October 2025.

The commission was set up by the UN Human Rights Council in 2021 and is chaired by Srinivasan Muralidhar. Its members are independent experts; they are not UN staff and are not paid for the work. This report concentrates on killings and on bodily and mental harm attributed to state actors, and devotes most of its new material to the West Bank.

There, the commission says, the situation has deteriorated gravely since 7 October 2023. One of its statistics concerns air power: six Palestinians were killed by air strikes in the West Bank between 2008 and 6 October 2023, and 254 between 7 October 2023 and 30 September 2026. It concludes that military operations and sustained settler violence together amount to a widespread and systematic attack on civilians, and that the authorities intended them to work in concert.

The report describes military operations since January 2025 as having caused the largest displacement of Palestinians in the West Bank since 1967, with 34,176 people still displaced in August 2026. It finds that children have been deliberately killed and harmed, which it classes as persecution on grounds of age, and that sexual violence has been used to displace and subjugate.

On Gaza, the commission says Israeli authorities and forces have continued to commit genocidal acts since the ceasefire, with intent to destroy Palestinians in whole or in part, and that the expansion of Israeli territorial control results in the killing of people who approach or cross the so-called yellow line. Muralidhar said the pattern forms part of a broader strategy to displace Palestinians and impose unliveable conditions.

Israel rejected the report. Its permanent mission in Geneva called it yet another manifestation of a misinformation campaign to demonise the State of Israel. Israel does not cooperate with the commission and has long accused it of bias. The commission's findings are not binding; it recommends that Israel comply with the International Court of Justice's advisory opinion of July 2024, end the occupation, remove settlements and return confiscated land to its owners.
