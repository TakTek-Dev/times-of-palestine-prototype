Palestinian kickboxers bring home two golds and six bronzes from the World Cup in Tashkent

In one line

Abdullah Kanaan and Mohammad Alyan won gold as Palestine's 27-strong team took eight medals at the kickboxing World Cup in Uzbekistan.

On record19 October 2026, 15:08 JerusalemTashkent2 min read

Palestine's kickboxing team finished the World Cup for national teams and clubs in Tashkent with two gold medals and six bronze, WAFA reported on 19 October. The tournament in the Uzbek capital brought together around 3,000 athletes from 77 teams. Abdullah Kanaan won gold in the senior category and Mohammad Alyan took gold among the juniors, giving the team a title at each level.

The six bronze medals went to Mustafa Sharaf, Anas al-Qaq, Mohammad Oweisat, Ahmad Sbeih, Yahya Jabr and Waad Bkeirat. The delegation numbered 27 male and female athletes and was led by Nizar Taleb, vice-president of the Palestinian Kickboxing Federation, with federation board members Tamer Bakr and Wasim Shamlawi on the administrative staff. WAFA did not list the weight classes in which the medals were won.

The squad was drawn from seven clubs and training centres, a list that reads like a map of the West Bank and Jerusalem: Aswar Beit al-Maqdis Club, the Al-Bustan Silwan Association, Be Strong Gym, Silwad Sports Club, Meithalun Sports Club, the Champions Center for Martial Arts and Target One MMA. Silwad lies north-east of Ramallah and Meithalun south of Jenin; al-Bustan is a neighbourhood of Silwan, in East Jerusalem.

Palestine's ambassador to Uzbekistan, Wael al-Batrikhi, congratulated the federation and put the result in the context of the year. Across five international competitions in 2026, he said, the national team has won 17 gold, 18 silver and 14 bronze medals. The federation says taking part in such events is part of a plan to develop the national squads and raise Palestine's presence in international sport.

It was a good week for Palestinian combat sports. On 18 October Maryam Bsharat won gold in the junior women's under-66kg kumite at the World Cadet, Junior and Under-21 Karate Championships in Poland, according to a chronology of the year's events. The two results came within twenty-four hours of each other, on different continents, in two different martial arts and at two different age levels.

The clubs behind the medals work in difficult conditions. Less than three weeks later, on 5 November, Israeli authorities closed the Sons of Jerusalem Club, a sports and social club in the Old City, by ministerial order, and on 10 November police renewed the closure of the Burj al-Laqlaq youth centre for six months. The Palestinian Authority's Jerusalem Governorate described both steps as narrowing the space for sport in the city.
