Who was Yasser Arafat, and how did Palestinians mark the 22nd anniversary of his death this week?

The short answer

The PLO chairman and first Palestinian president died on 11 November 2004; a wreath was laid at his Ramallah tomb on Wednesday.

Today11 November 2026, 11:53 JerusalemRamallah2 min read

Palestinian Vice-President Hussein al-Sheikh laid a wreath at the tomb of Yasser Arafat in the presidential compound in Ramallah on Wednesday 11 November, on behalf of President Mahmoud Abbas, WAFA reported. It was the 22nd anniversary of Arafat's death. Al-Sheikh recited verses from the Quran at the graveside. The ceremony is held every year at the mausoleum inside the compound known as the Muqata'a.

Arafat, known as Abu Ammar, was born in 1929. He was among the founders of the Fatah movement in the late 1950s and became chairman of the Palestine Liberation Organization in February 1969, a post he held until his death. In 1974 he addressed the UN General Assembly, where he spoke of carrying an olive branch and a freedom fighter's gun. In November 1988 the PLO under his leadership declared the State of Palestine.

He signed the Oslo accords with Israel in 1993 and shared the 1994 Nobel Peace Prize with Yitzhak Rabin and Shimon Peres. That year he returned from exile and settled in Gaza City as head of the new Palestinian Authority. The peace process stalled; during the second intifada the Israeli army confined him to his Ramallah compound for more than two years.

He died on 11 November 2004 in a military hospital at Clamart, near Paris, aged 75. The cause has been disputed ever since. Russian and French investigations found no evidence of foul play, while a Swiss team concluded that he had been poisoned with radioactive polonium, according to the summary of the inquiries on Wikipedia. He is buried in a mausoleum inside the compound where he spent his last years.

In a statement the day before the anniversary, Abbas said honouring Arafat meant holding to the principles he died for: self-determination, the return of refugees, and an independent state on the 1967 lines with East Jerusalem as its capital. He referred to Arafat's death as an assassination, the official Palestinian position, and said it had not broken the will of the Palestinian people.

Abbas used the occasion to address present politics. He called national unity an urgent priority, described the PLO as the sole legitimate representative of the Palestinian people, and urged all factions to end the division under the formula of one authority, one law and one legitimate weapon. He also pledged to press for aid to enter Gaza, for reconstruction and against displacement. Presidential and legislative elections that had been planned were postponed in October, according to a chronology of the year's events.
