New international volume documents the fate of Palestinian libraries and archives from 1948 to 1993

In one line

'Resisting Erasure', from Library Juice Press, includes Anan Hamad's study of Palestinian libraries and archives under occupation, 1948-1993.

On record19 October 2026, 15:31 JerusalemRamallah2 min read

A new book from the international publisher Library Juice Press sets out to document what has happened to Palestinian libraries and archives over the past three quarters of a century. 'Resisting Erasure: Libraries in Palestine and Palestine in Libraries' includes a full-length study by the Palestinian researcher Anan Hamad, 'History of Palestinian Libraries and Archives Under Israeli Occupation (1948-1993)', WAFA reported on 19 October.

Hamad's study follows libraries and archival collections from their origins through the decades after 1948, up to the year of the Oslo accords. It examines how they were founded and developed, the obstacles they faced under Israeli occupation, and the targeting and looting of major intellectual and archival collections, according to the agency's description of the work. It covers 45 years in all.

The volume as a whole looks in two directions, as its title suggests: at libraries inside Palestine, and at how Palestine is represented, catalogued and collected in libraries elsewhere. For researchers abroad and for Palestinians in the diaspora who depend on foreign collections to study their own history, the second question is as practical as the first, because it decides what can be found.

WAFA describes the study as a contribution to documenting national memory and preserving cultural and intellectual heritage, and as a reference for future generations of researchers interested in the country's history. Including it in full in an English-language academic book, the agency notes, places Palestinian knowledge resources before an international readership that might not otherwise encounter research by Palestinian scholars.

The book appears at a time of heavy losses to cultural institutions. The UN's special rapporteur on the occupied Palestinian territory, Francesca Albanese, told reporters on 27 October that about 150 media centres and offices in Gaza have been destroyed, some 80 per cent of the territory's media infrastructure. The Palestinian delegate to UNESCO told its executive board in October that education and culture in Gaza had been hit across the organisation's whole mandate.

Interest in the field is growing in universities. On the day the book was announced, the University of Barcelona launched Spain's first chair in Palestine studies, with research on heritage among its stated aims. Hamad's history ends in 1993; the story of what has happened to Palestinian libraries, archives and their catalogues since then, including during the past three years in Gaza, is still being written.
