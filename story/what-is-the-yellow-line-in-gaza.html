What is the 'yellow line' in Gaza, and why does the UN say people near it keep being displaced?

The short answer

It is the line Israeli forces pulled back to under the October 2025 ceasefire; the UN says it is shifting west and treated as a kill zone.

On record30 October 2026, 15:00 JerusalemGaza City2 min read

The yellow line is the boundary to which Israeli forces withdrew under the ceasefire agreement of October 2025. East of it the army remains deployed; west of it is the shrinking strip where most of Gaza's population now lives. The UN humanitarian office has described the line as largely unmarked on the ground, although its latest reports mention new yellow concrete blocks appearing in Ash Shuja'iyyeh and in parts of Deir al-Balah.

The UN human rights office says the line is moving. In a statement on 30 October it reported that Israel has declared an aim of expanding its military deployment area to 70 per cent of Gaza's territory, and that over the previous two weeks its monitors documented westward expansion in Shuja'iyyeh and Gaza City and at three points in central Gaza: eastern Al Bureij, eastern Deir al-Balah and eastern Al Maghazi.

For families camped nearby, that means moving again. The office said it received reports that on 25 October a drone dropped munitions on tents at Shuja'iyyeh junction, starting a fire that burned for five hours. The Palestinian Ministry of Public Works and Housing said 200 families, about 800 people, were displaced. UN teams who visited the area days later found that more tents had been set alight, leaving at least nine households without shelter.

Ajith Sunghay, who heads the UN human rights office in the occupied Palestinian territory, called displacement in Gaza both acute and chronic. People uprooted before the ceasefire have little prospect of going home, he said, because most of their neighbourhoods lie beyond the line. His office said Israeli forces appear to treat the line as a kill zone, and that forcible transfer within occupied territory is a war crime.

An independent UN Commission of Inquiry reached a similar conclusion in a report presented to the General Assembly on 26 October. Its chair, Srinivasan Muralidhar, said Israel's expansion of territorial control continues to result in the killing and harming of Palestinians who approach or cross the line. Israel rejected the commission's findings; its mission in Geneva called the report part of a misinformation campaign to demonise the state.

The Gaza Ministry of Health counted 36 people killed and 145 injured between 27 October and 2 November, and 1,593 killed since the ceasefire took effect, according to OCHA. UN staff have been caught up too: the organisation reported shooting near three UN schools and a convoy in late October, and said staff had to take cover from gunfire west of the line in Gaza City on 7 November.
