GazaWinter storm

First winter storm floods displacement sites across Gaza, damaging more than 23,000 family shelters

In one line

OCHA says rain from 6 November destroyed over 6,000 family shelters, damaged 17,400 more and left 3,900 households with none.

This week8 November 2026, 10:00 JerusalemGaza Strip2 min read

Heavy rain and strong winds that began on the evening of 6 November flooded displacement sites in all five governorates of the Gaza Strip, according to a situation report published by the UN humanitarian office OCHA. Preliminary figures gathered by the Site Management Cluster up to midnight on 7 November recorded about 268 weather alerts affecting more than 26,000 households. Over 6,000 family shelters were reported destroyed and 17,400 damaged, and more than 3,900 households were left with no shelter at all.

OCHA estimates that about 65 per cent of Gaza's population, roughly 1.4 million people, still live in tents or makeshift shelters. Civil Defense said water reached 1.5 metres in some places on 7 November. Its emergency rooms logged 2,166 distress calls in a single day, but rescue teams could physically reach only 218 of them; most callers were given advice over the phone. Gaza Municipality received another 251 incident reports and pointed to wrecked drainage networks and a lack of pumps and heavy machinery.

The official Palestinian news agency WAFA reported that rainwater poured into thousands of tents pitched on low ground and that two people were injured in northern Gaza when the wall of a building already damaged by bombardment gave way. The agency put the number of tents in use across the Strip at about 270,000, with more than 400,000 displaced people living in places exposed to flooding or close to the shoreline. Aid partners have identified at least 4,000 buildings at high risk of collapse.

Services were hit as well. OCHA said at least 55 learning spaces serving about 55,000 children were affected, and classes were suspended in 20 that were damaged. Eight community kitchens halved their output on 7 November, which meant about 25,000 fewer meals that day, before returning to normal on 8 November. Water entered departments at Nasser Medical Complex, the basement of the Patient's Friends Benevolent Society Hospital was flooded, and several partners suspended mobile clinics for a day or more.

Relief teams installed 50,000 sandbags over two days, distributed winter clothes to 3,364 households in Khan Younis and Deir al-Balah, and operated 15 dewatering pumps. By 8 November heavy machinery was supporting 200 affected sites. OCHA nonetheless described partners as overwhelmed by appeals. It said stocks of tents, tarpaulins and blankets inside Gaza are nearly exhausted, while more than 150,000 tents, 300,000 tarpaulins and 500,000 blankets are waiting outside the territory.

The report lists three urgent needs: Israeli approval to bring in shelter items, engine oil, spare parts and heavy machinery; vacuum trucks, mobile pumps and hoses; and more humanitarian funding. Mine action teams added a separate warning that floodwater can uncover unexploded ordnance or carry it into areas previously considered clear. OCHA stressed that all its figures are preliminary and subject to verification as assessments continue across the affected sites.
