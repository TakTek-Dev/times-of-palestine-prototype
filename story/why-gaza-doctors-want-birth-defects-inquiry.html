Why are doctors in Gaza asking for an independent inquiry into a rise in birth defects?

The short answer

Gaza recorded 260 congenital anomalies in nine months of 2026 against 184 in all of 2022, and doctors say the cause has not been established.

This week6 November 2026, 16:14 JerusalemGaza Strip2 min read

A medical source in Gaza told WAFA on 6 November that 260 cases of congenital anomalies were recorded among newborns in the first nine months of 2026. That is already more than the 184 cases reported in the whole of 2022, the last full year before the war. The same source said 322 cases were recorded in 2025, an increase of 75 per cent on 2022, and asked that the trend be examined by outside specialists.

The source was careful about what the figures do and do not show. Raw case counts, the report notes, do not establish the rate of anomalies among all births and do not identify a cause. That caution is the reason for the request: the source called for an independent international scientific investigation, something beyond the capacity of a health system that, by the Red Crescent's account this week, currently struggles to run even basic laboratory tests.

Three possible factors were named for investigators to examine: malnutrition among mothers, exposure to environmental pollutants, and the disruption of antenatal care during the war. The conditions being recorded include cleft lip and palate, shortened upper limbs, umbilical hernias and ambiguous external genitalia that make it hard to determine a baby's sex at birth. One child may have more than one anomaly, the source said, so the number of affected babies may be lower than the case count.

Other data point to the strain on mothers and babies. OCHA's report of 5 November, citing the Health Cluster, says neonatal units in Gaza ran at 151 per cent occupancy in September and special care baby units at 199 per cent, and that nearly 63 per cent of deliveries were classed as high-risk. The cluster estimates that between 123 and 179 additional neonatal beds are needed in the last quarter of the year.

The environmental question is also open. In a statement on 6 November the Palestinian Environmental Quality Authority estimated that Gaza holds between 61 and 68 million tons of rubble, about four million tons of hazardous waste and at least 50,000 tons of asbestos, and that around 120,000 cubic metres of untreated wastewater is released every day. The authority's figures are its own estimates and have not been independently verified.

WAFA's report recalls that Human Rights Watch documented serious risks to pregnant women in Gaza in a report published on 28 January 2025, pointing to the blockade, the destruction of health facilities and shortages of food, water and medicine. Aid partners say they have bought postpartum kits and warming items for newborns this winter. Whether the rise in recorded anomalies reflects real change, better reporting or both is exactly what the doctors want an outside inquiry to settle.
