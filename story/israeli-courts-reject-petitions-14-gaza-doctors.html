Israeli courts reject two petitions seeking the release of 14 Gaza doctors held without charge

In one line

Physicians for Human Rights-Israel says the 14 doctors, some held for more than two years, include hospital directors and specialists.

This week5 November 2026, 14:00 JerusalemGaza Strip2 min read

Israeli courts have rejected two petitions filed by the rights group Physicians for Human Rights-Israel for the release of 14 Palestinian doctors from Gaza, WAFA reported in a dispatch dated 5 November. The organisation says the doctors are being held in Israeli prisons without charge or trial, and that some of them have been in detention for more than two years. The report does not name the doctors or give details of the judges' reasoning.

Naji Abbas of Physicians for Human Rights-Israel said the group includes hospital directors and specialists in critical fields of medicine. In his view the two rulings show the scale of the violations against detained medical staff and the failure of the Israeli judiciary to protect their rights. Detainees, he argued, should not lose their medical rights because of their public profile or because of security allegations made against them.

Abbas framed the case as a matter of patient care as well as individual liberty. Israel cannot continue to hold doctors without trial, he said, because it deprives patients in Gaza of their care while denying the doctors themselves basic medical treatment. The organisation's petitions asked for the doctors to be released; it was not clear from the report whether it intends to take the cases further or to file new applications.

The staffing gap is acknowledged across the health sector. When Japan and the World Health Organization signed a $1.97 million grant on 10 November, the announcement said Gaza's health system faces immense pressure from shortages of health personnel, growing patient needs and rising demand for specialised services. Part of the money will train 100 local emergency medical team members so that Gaza relies less on visiting foreign teams.

Detention conditions are a concern in their own right. The Palestinian Prisoners' Society said on 8 November that testimony gathered by its lawyers in October and early November indicated about 80 per cent of detainees had scabies, which it linked to a severe shortage of clothing and a lack of treatment. Palestinian detainee bodies put the number of people held in Israeli prisons at more than 9,300, including about 90 women.

Israel says it detains people from Gaza on security grounds; the petitions concerned doctors against whom, according to the rights group, no charges have been brought. A WAFA feature published on 1 November described a similar pattern in the West Bank, profiling a 71-year-old general practitioner, Mazen al-Rantisi, who was held for four months and who said nine other doctors were detained in the same period.
