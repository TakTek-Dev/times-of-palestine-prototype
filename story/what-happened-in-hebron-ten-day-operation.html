West BankHebron

What happened during Israel's ten-day operation in Hebron, and what did families find when it ended?

The short answer

From 23 October to 2 November Israeli forces sealed two Hebron neighbourhoods, displacing more than 3,000 people who are now returning.

On record2 November 2026, 18:00 JerusalemHebron2 min read

It began with a shooting. On Friday 23 October two Palestinian gunmen opened fire on an Israeli military checkpoint at the edge of Hebron, killing two soldiers and wounding several others before being shot dead. Within hours Israeli forces had sealed the Jildah and Ein Deir Bahah neighbourhoods and imposed sweeping movement restrictions on the city and its surroundings. The operation lasted ten days and ended on 2 November, according to OCHA.

Counts of the displaced rose through the week. The UN said on 26 October that more than 3,000 people had been evacuated by Israeli forces. On 29 October Hebron governor Khaled Dudin put the figure at 537 families, or 3,361 people, among them 232 infants, 326 older people and 147 pregnant or breastfeeding women. By 30 October WAFA reported about 5,000 people either displaced or confined to their homes. OCHA notes that the municipality's figures are still being verified.

Daily life stopped well beyond the two neighbourhoods. OCHA says about 90,000 students in the Hebron education directorate were affected by a temporary suspension of in-person teaching, and 11 schools with some 4,450 pupils remained out of reach until 3 November. The Hebron Chamber of Commerce estimated losses of 10 to 12 million shekels a day. Palestinian sources reported more than 115 arrests; WAFA described a protest by displaced families at the closed Jildah roundabout on 30 October.

On 30 October Israeli forces used explosives to demolish two apartments belonging to the families of the two attackers. OCHA says three families, 15 people including nine children, lost their homes, neighbouring flats were damaged and eight relatives were arrested. Such punitive demolitions accounted for seven of the 31 Palestinian-owned structures destroyed across the West Bank during that week, the office said.

The UN's human rights chief, Volker Türk, said on 26 October that the operation raised grave concerns of unnecessary and disproportionate force and that collective punishment is a grave violation of international law. He said threats by senior officials to empty neighbourhoods raised serious concerns of ethnic cleansing. Sixty members of the European Parliament wrote to the EU's foreign policy chief calling the closure collective punishment. Israel said it was acting against those behind a deadly attack.

Families started going back after 2 November. OCHA reports damage to belongings, including in homes that had reportedly been used as military positions. Aid agencies and the municipality have given one round of emergency cash, about 500 food parcels, blankets, mattresses and hygiene kits, and a second cash round is being considered. Mobile clinics were still suspended in 10 places in the governorate on 3 November. A delegation of UN and NGO heads visited the city the day the operation ended.
