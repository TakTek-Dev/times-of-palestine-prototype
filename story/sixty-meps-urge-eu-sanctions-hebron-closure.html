Sixty members of the European Parliament urge emergency EU meeting on sanctions over Hebron closure

In one line

In a letter to EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas, 60 MEPs called the West Bank closure collective punishment and asked for sanctions talks.

On record27 October 2026, 11:07 JerusalemBrussels2 min read

Sixty members of the European Parliament have asked the EU's foreign policy chief, Kaja Kallas, to convene an urgent meeting of the bloc's foreign ministers to agree sanctions on the Israeli government. The request came in a letter sent on the evening of Monday 26 October and signed by lawmakers from several of the parliament's political groups, WAFA reported the following day.

The trigger was the closure imposed on the West Bank after two Israeli soldiers were shot dead at a checkpoint near Hebron on 23 October. The letter describes the measures as collective punishment, which it says constitutes a war crime under international law, and accuses the Israeli government of using them to consolidate its occupation and to raise tensions during Israel's election campaign. Polling day was 27 October.

The signatories cite the demolition of homes in Hebron and say some families were given no more than five minutes to leave. They call the events a decisive turning point for the future of the West Bank and for the viability of a Palestinian state, and say the European Union cannot remain silent in the face of what they describe as a large-scale assault.

Their demands are threefold: that the EU condemn the measures, that it call for the withdrawal of Israeli forces from the West Bank, and that foreign ministers meet urgently to reach agreement on sanctions. Sanctions require unanimity among the 27 member states, which is why the letter asks for a ministerial meeting and not for a decision by Kallas herself.

By the time the letter was sent, according to figures from Hebron officials quoted by WAFA, 619 families had been affected by the operation, about 3,971 people had been displaced and 85 arrested. The UN human rights chief, Volker Türk, said the same day that the operation raised grave concerns of disproportionate force and that collective punishment is a grave violation of international law.

The EU has not announced such a meeting. On 10 November it repeated its demand that Israel ensure the delivery of humanitarian aid to Gaza, WAFA reported. Other governments have moved separately: the British foreign secretary said in October that the UK had created powers to sanction companies involved in building settlements in the E1 area, and twelve countries announced restrictions on settlement products in September, according to a chronology of the year.
