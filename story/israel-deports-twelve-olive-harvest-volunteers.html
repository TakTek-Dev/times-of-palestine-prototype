Israel orders deportation of 12 foreign volunteers detained while helping with olive harvest near Ramallah

In one line

Seventeen people were detained in Kafr Ni'ma on 6 November; five Germans, four Italians, two Canadians and an American were ordered deported.

This week6 November 2026, 19:29 JerusalemKafr Ni'ma2 min read

Israeli authorities decided to deport 12 international volunteers who were detained on Friday 6 November while helping Palestinian farmers pick olives in the village of Kafr Ni'ma, west of Ramallah, according to the Faza'a campaign, which organises volunteers for the harvest. The group said a lawyer following the case had been told the activists would be removed on Saturday morning. They are five Germans, four Italians, two Canadians and one American.

Seventeen people were detained in all. Besides the 12 volunteers there were two foreign journalists and three Palestinians, one of them a woman. Faza'a said the three Palestinians were its Ramallah coordinator and two farmers, and that soldiers assaulted the farmers before taking them away. The detainees were first taken to a nearby military post, the campaign said in its statement.

The campaign's account, carried by WAFA, is that a group of settlers attacked the pickers near Jabal al-Risan, on the edge of the village, and held them by force, and that the army then detained those who had been attacked. There was no account of the incident from the Israeli military in the reports available. The same day, soldiers expelled foreign activists and journalists from a village north-east of Ramallah, the agency reported.

It was not the first such case this season. Faza'a said several foreign activists were detained in Masafer Yatta, south of Hebron, on 22 October and later deported. Israel's settler leadership said in early October that the defence minister, Israel Katz, had ordered a ban on pro-Palestinian activists assisting olive harvesters in the West Bank, according to a chronology of the year's events.

Faza'a condemned the deportations as an attempt to silence witnesses to settler attacks, to prevent violations from being documented and to obstruct international efforts to help farmers reach their land. It said it would continue its activities across the West Bank with Palestinian and international volunteers for the rest of the season, to support farmers and strengthen their presence on their land.

The UN describes the presence of volunteers as one of the few protective measures available. OCHA said this month that protection partners were supporting volunteer harvest campaigns and providing a protective presence, but that help reaches only a limited number of pickers. Volunteers have themselves been hurt: two Israeli solidarity activists were injured by settlers near Aqraba on 1 November, and a protective-presence volunteer was injured in Kafr Malik, the office reported.
