What is UNRWA, and why are refugees in the West Bank protesting against cuts to its services?

The short answer

The UN agency for Palestine refugees faces a shortfall of about $70 million and is weighing layoffs and the closure of its Qalqilya hospital.

This week7 November 2026, 09:16 JerusalemNablus2 min read

UNRWA is the United Nations Relief and Works Agency for Palestine Refugees in the Near East. The General Assembly created it by Resolution 302 after the 1948 war to assist Palestinians who had lost their homes, and its mandate has been renewed ever since in the absence of a political settlement. It runs schools, clinics and relief programmes for registered refugees in Gaza, the West Bank including East Jerusalem, Jordan, Lebanon and Syria.

Unlike most UN bodies, UNRWA delivers services directly, and almost all of its staff are Palestinian teachers, doctors, nurses and sanitation workers. It depends on voluntary contributions from governments. That combination makes it sensitive to any drop in funding: when donors hold back, the effect is felt within weeks in classrooms and health centres, and in the salaries of its own employees.

That is the situation now. The PLO Executive Committee said on 25 October that about $70 million is needed urgently to meet immediate needs and pay staff salaries for November and December. It said the agency's management was considering large-scale layoffs, which it rejected, arguing that staff are the backbone of the agency and that dismissing them would not solve the crisis. It urged the acting commissioner-general to tour donor capitals.

The cuts are already visible in the West Bank. UNRWA closed the internal medicine ward of its hospital in Qalqilya in December 2025 and the maternity ward in March 2026, and has decided to close the 63-bed hospital altogether, according to WAFA. Refugee committees protested in Balata camp, in Nablus, on 27 October and in the hospital courtyard on 10 November. A PLO official put the cost of keeping the hospital open at an additional $3 million.

The agency is under pressure on other fronts too. In Gaza, OCHA reported in late 2025 that Israeli authorities were barring UNRWA from bringing aid in directly, even as its schools sheltered tens of thousands of displaced people. In the northern West Bank, tens of thousands of refugees remain displaced from the Jenin, Tulkarm and Nur Shams camps after Israeli military operations that began in early 2025; a UN inquiry put the figure at 34,176 in August.

Diplomatic support has not translated into money. UNESCO's executive board unanimously adopted a resolution in October supporting the continuity of UNRWA's education work, and on 6 November the UN secretary-general, António Guterres, reaffirmed his full commitment to defending the agency and called for it to be fully funded. The PLO has proposed a joint committee with the host countries to lead a funding campaign. Until donors respond, the choice in front of the agency is which services to stop.
