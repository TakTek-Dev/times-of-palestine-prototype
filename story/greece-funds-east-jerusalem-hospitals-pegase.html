Greece commits €600,000 to East Jerusalem hospitals as WHO data show a quarter of patient permits unmet

In one line

Greece signed a €600,000 grant for East Jerusalem hospitals on 2 November; WHO says 76% of patient permit requests were approved this year.

On record2 November 2026, 17:44 JerusalemEast Jerusalem2 min read

Greece has committed €600,000 to the hospitals of East Jerusalem. The agreement was signed on Monday 2 November by the Greek consul general in Jerusalem, Dimitrios Angelosopoulos, and the Palestinian Authority's minister of finance and planning, Estephan Salameh, WAFA reported. The money will be paid through PEGASE, the direct financial support mechanism set up by the European Commission and run by the office of the EU representative in Jerusalem.

The East Jerusalem hospitals are a network of Palestinian charitable and church-run institutions, among them Augusta Victoria on the Mount of Olives and Makassed, that provide specialised treatment not available elsewhere in the Palestinian health system. They treat patients referred from across the West Bank and Gaza by the Palestinian Authority, which is why donor support for them is routed through an agreement with the authority's finance ministry. WAFA described the Greek money as a contribution to essential health care services.

Getting to the hospitals is its own obstacle. Patients from the rest of the West Bank and from Gaza need an Israeli permit to enter East Jerusalem. The World Health Organization reports that 76 per cent of 93,051 patient permit applications for treatment in East Jerusalem or Israel were approved between January and October 2026, according to OCHA. That is an improvement on 64 per cent in 2025 but still below the level before October 2023.

The remaining quarter matters, the UN office notes, because applications that were denied or still pending were not resolved by the date of the patient's appointment. A permit that arrives after the appointment date is, in practice, a missed treatment session or a postponed operation, whatever the eventual decision. OCHA adds that 872 health service points were functioning across the West Bank in October, 490 of them only partially.

The road in is slow even with a permit. On the evening of 1 November WAFA reported long queues at the Qalandia checkpoint and the nearby roundabout in Kafr Aqab, and at the Shu'fat refugee camp checkpoint, after Israeli forces tightened procedures, and a temporary checkpoint at the entrance to Hizma. On 9 November the entrances to Hizma were closed altogether.

Greece is not alone in directing money at Palestinian health care this month. Japan signed a $1.97 million grant with the WHO on 10 November for emergency medical coordination in Gaza, and Arab health ministers meeting the same day called for continued support to the Palestinian health sector. For the Jerusalem hospitals the Greek contribution is modest in scale, but it arrives at a moment when UN agencies report that funding for health and relief work is shrinking.
