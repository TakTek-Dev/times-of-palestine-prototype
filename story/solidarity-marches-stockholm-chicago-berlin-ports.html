SolidarityMarches

From Stockholm's Odenplan to the Port of Elizabeth: five solidarity actions of the past three weeks

In one line

Marches in Stockholm, Chicago, Berlin and Hanover, a port protest in New Jersey and a cross-country US convoy all called for an end to arms flows.

This week8 November 2026, 09:25 JerusalemStockholm2 min read

Stockholm, 7 November. Hundreds of people gathered at Odenplan square on Saturday evening after a call from several civil society organisations, WAFA reported. They protested against Israeli attacks on Gaza, the West Bank and Lebanon and against violations of the ceasefire. Placards referred to children killed in Gaza, to schools and hospitals, and to food shortages. The demonstrators asked the Swedish government to suspend arms sales to Israel.

Chicago, reported 9 November. A march through the city brought together activists calling for an end to the war in Gaza and to US support for Israel, according to the agency's report, which did not give the exact date or a crowd estimate. Marchers carried Palestinian flags. The demonstration also took up the rights of immigrants in the United States, and speakers stressed the importance of sustained local organising to put pressure on the administration in Washington.

Berlin and Hanover, 25 October. Demonstrations were held in several German cities on the Sunday after Israel sealed the West Bank. Hundreds marched in Hanover with Palestinian flags and watermelon symbols, and hundreds more in Berlin, where speakers condemned what they called starvation policies in Gaza and the escalating violence in the West Bank. Participants called for an immediate end to the war and for the protection of civilians.

Port of Elizabeth, New Jersey, 1 November. Activists drove in procession through the port area to oppose the export and transfer of weapons to Israel and called for a boycott of the Israeli shipping line Zim. Organisers said the action was one of a series held that week at more than 100 ports in at least 22 countries, involving port workers and trade unions as well as campaigners. Those figures are the organisers' own.

Los Angeles to Washington, 17 to 26 October. A convoy of 42 people calling itself the 2026 Freedom Convoy USA set out from the Israeli consulate in Los Angeles on a ten-day drive across the country. Its route ran through Las Vegas, Albuquerque, Tulsa, St Louis and Chicago, among other stops. It planned to deliver a petition titled 'We the People' to congressional offices ahead of the 3 November midterm elections.

The convoy's participants included members of religious groups, former government employees and military veterans, WAFA reported. Their argument was framed around domestic priorities: money spent on military support for Israel, they said, should go to health care, housing, education and childcare. On election day itself President Donald Trump said in a television interview that annual US funding to Israel, about $3.8 billion, would end this year under an agreement with Benjamin Netanyahu.

The common thread in all five is arms. Whether in a Swedish square, an American port or a convoy crossing the Midwest, the demand addressed to governments was the same: stop supplying weapons. The crowds described in the reports were in the hundreds in Stockholm, Berlin and Hanover, far smaller than the mass marches of 2023 and 2024, but the actions were spread across at least three countries in three weeks.
