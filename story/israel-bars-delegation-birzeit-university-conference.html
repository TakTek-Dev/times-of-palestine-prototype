Israel turns back 23 foreign parliamentarians and academics bound for Birzeit University conference

In one line

A delegation including British MP Clive Lewis was questioned for hours at the Allenby crossing on 27 October and refused entry.

On record27 October 2026, 11:13 JerusalemBirzeit2 min read

Israeli authorities refused entry to the West Bank on Tuesday 27 October to an international delegation of 23 parliamentarians, politicians and academics who were travelling to a conference at Birzeit University, the university said. The group arrived at the Allenby crossing, the land crossing between Jordan and the West Bank, which Israel controls. According to the organisers, its members were held and questioned for several hours before being turned back.

Birzeit named several of those refused entry: the British Labour MP Clive Lewis; Lara Bird of the Scottish National Party; Kate Nevens, a Scottish Green member of the Scottish Parliament; the Spanish senator Josu Estarrona Elizondo of EH Bildu; and the former British MP Sam Tarry. The full delegation came from several countries, the university's statement said, without listing every name.

They had been invited to a conference organised by the university's Ibrahim Abu-Lughod Institute of International Studies under the title 'Global South Solidarity and Resistance to Imperialism', which opened the same day. Their programme also included meetings with students, academics and civil society organisations, and joining activities in support of farmers during the olive harvest, at a time of escalating settler attacks.

The date was not incidental. Israel had imposed a full closure on the West Bank after a deadly shooting near Hebron four days earlier, and 27 October was election day in Israel. There was no statement from the Israeli authorities on the reasons for the refusal in the reports available. Israel controls all entry to the West Bank and has in recent years denied entry to a number of foreign politicians critical of its policies.

The university said the refusal fits a pattern of restrictions on Palestinian academic institutions and on their contact with scholars, parliamentarians and delegations from abroad. Such restrictions, it said, obstruct academic exchange and prevent visitors from seeing conditions under occupation for themselves. It called for the protection of academic freedom and of universities' ability to work with partners and supporters internationally.

The conference was due to open the same day. The previous evening, 60 members of the European Parliament had sent a letter calling for EU sanctions over the Hebron closure, and on 6 November Israel ordered the deportation of 12 foreign volunteers detained while helping with the olive harvest near Ramallah. Birzeit, north of Ramallah, is one of the oldest Palestinian universities.
