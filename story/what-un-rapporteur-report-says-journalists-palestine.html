What does the UN rapporteur's new report say about the killing and jailing of Palestinian journalists?

The short answer

Francesca Albanese's report says nearly 300 journalists and media workers have been killed in Gaza and 80% of its media offices destroyed.

On record27 October 2026, 19:00 JerusalemGaza Strip2 min read

The UN special rapporteur on the occupied Palestinian territory, Francesca Albanese, presented a report on the media to journalists on Tuesday 27 October. Titled 'Truth under the rubble: media in the time of genocide', it was submitted to the General Assembly. She briefed reporters by video link because US sanctions imposed on her prevent her from travelling to New York. Special rapporteurs are independent experts appointed by the Human Rights Council and do not speak for the UN.

Her central figures concern Gaza. Citing UN sources, Albanese said nearly 300 journalists and media workers have been killed there since October 2023, more than 100 have been detained, many of them tortured, and about 150 media centres and offices have been destroyed, which she put at 80 per cent of Gaza's media infrastructure. A statement by a group of UN experts on 2 November added that more than 400 have been wounded.

The report identifies around 70 journalists who, it alleges, were killed in reprisal for their work, the experts' statement said. It calls for independent, prompt and effective investigations and for journalists' material to be preserved as potential evidence. Israel has denied targeting journalists as such. Counting is itself contested: in September the Committee to Protect Journalists removed 22 names from its list of journalists killed in Gaza after they were identified as militants, according to a chronology of the year's events.

The West Bank receives its own chapter. Albanese said more than 139 attacks on journalists have recently been recorded there. Reporters covering this autumn's olive harvest have been among those affected: two foreign journalists were among 17 people detained by Israeli forces in Kafr Ni'ma on 6 November, WAFA reported, and a journalist was injured in a settler attack south of Nablus on 10 November.

Albanese criticised Israel's continuing ban on foreign journalists entering Gaza independently, which leaves coverage dependent on local reporters. She also raised the case of Shireen Abu Akleh, the Al Jazeera correspondent shot dead in Jenin in May 2022, for whose killing no one has been held to account. Asked for an update on accountability, she answered that the update is impunity.

The report is not limited to Israel. It documents intimidation and repression of Palestinian journalists by Hamas in Gaza and by the Palestinian Authority in the West Bank. The wider context was set out by the UN experts on the international day against impunity for crimes against journalists: according to UNESCO, more than 1,800 journalists were killed worldwide between 2006 and 2025, and close to nine in ten of those killings remain unresolved in court.
