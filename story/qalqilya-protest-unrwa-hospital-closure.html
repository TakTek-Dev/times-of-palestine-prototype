Qalqilya residents rally in hospital courtyard against UNRWA plan to close the 63-bed facility

In one line

Protesters want UNRWA to keep its Qalqilya hospital, open since 1951 and serving more than 17,000 refugee families, and reopen two wards.

Today10 November 2026, 20:24 JerusalemQalqilya2 min read

Officials, refugee committee members and residents gathered on Tuesday 10 November in the courtyard of the UNRWA hospital in Qalqilya to protest against the agency's decision to close it. The hospital, opened in 1951, has 63 beds and serves more than 17,000 refugee families in the governorate, according to figures reported by WAFA. The demonstration brought together representatives of official institutions, the Popular Committee for Refugee Services and the local community.

Mohammad Khader, speaking for the governor of Qalqilya, called the hospital a vital health and humanitarian institution and said the responsibility for keeping it open could not be set aside for financial or administrative reasons. The protesters' demands were specific: reverse the closure, reopen the internal medicine and maternity departments, and protect the jobs and rights of the staff who work there.

The wards have been closing in stages. UNRWA shut the internal medicine department in December 2025 and the maternity department in March 2026 as part of cost-cutting measures, WAFA reported. Abdul Rahim Jabr, who heads the Popular Committee for Refugee Services, said health care is a right of refugees, not a favour, and that peaceful protests would continue until the demands are met.

The rally was the second in a fortnight. On 27 October the PLO's Refugee Affairs Department and camp committees held a demonstration in Balata refugee camp, in Nablus, against service cuts, staff dismissals and the Qalqilya plan. Nisreen Thawqan, the department's director for the northern West Bank, said then that donors would be asked to cover a shortfall of about $70 million in UNRWA's budget and to find a further $3 million to keep the hospital running.

UNRWA has not publicly detailed its plans for the site in the reports available, and the agency's acting leadership is under pressure from several directions. The PLO Executive Committee said on 25 October that it rejected any large-scale layoffs and urged the acting commissioner-general to tour donor capitals. On 6 November UN Secretary-General António Guterres reaffirmed his full support for the agency and called for its work to be fully funded.

Access to care across the West Bank is already strained. The World Health Organization counts 872 functioning health service points, of which 490 work only partially and 22 others are out of service, according to OCHA's latest report, and mobile clinics run by aid groups cover 227 locations that have no fixed facility. For refugees in Qalqilya, speakers at the protest said, the question is whether a hospital that has treated them for 75 years will still be there next year.
